摘要

研究 Syracuse 奇数压缩映射 $S(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{v(n)}}$，$v(n)=\nu_2(3n+1)\ge1$，$n$ 为正奇数。要检验的直觉是：虽然一个区组里的首次除以 2 是必然的、倍率可能仍约为 $3/2$，但连续二分的累计量可能迫使系统在更长时间尺度上下降。

本文得到五项严格结论：

  1. 对自然密度意义下的奇数总体，$\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$；更强地，任意固定有限赋值字串的密度为 $2^{-\sum v_i}$，在奇数 2-adic Haar 概率空间中对应 i.i.d. 几何变量。
  2. 在简化倍率模型 $\mu=3\cdot2^{-v}$ 中，算术平均倍率为 1，对数漂移为 $\log_2 3-2<0$，且任意 $0<\theta<1$ 的分数阶矩满足 $\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]<1$。一阶矩临界不等于所有矩方法都会失败。
  3. 对真实轨迹有精确的下降判据：若此前尚未低于起点 $n_0$，而当前前缀的平均赋值超过 $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$，则当前末端必低于 $n_0$。
  4. 任何始终不低于起点 $n_0>1$ 的轨迹，在每个前缀都满足 $\frac1m\sum_{i<m}v_i\le\log_2(3+\frac1{n_0})<2$。这是任何最小反例或非平凡循环必须满足的"低累计二分"条件。
  5. 统计独立性与强大数定律只能说明典型 2-adic 初值不会长期满足上述低平均条件；正整数在 Haar 测度下是零测集，因此不能排除某个特殊正整数。缺少的正是把统计规律提升为对每个正整数的确定性控制。

此外，v0.1 第 7 节的比特预算需要修正：一个区组对应 $v_i$ 次 shortcut 迭代。$2.409\log_2 n$ 个区组对应约 $4.819\log_2 n$ 次 shortcut 迭代；若只使用约 $\log_2 n$ 个初始奇偶位，模型估计的剩余指数为 $\log_4 3\approx0.79248$，与 Korec 的经典结果一致。这是启发式的一致，不是新的证明。

§1定义与记号

为避免混淆"普通步数"和"奇数区组数"，使用两个映射。

1.1 shortcut 映射

$$C(n)=\begin{cases} n/2, & n\text{ 为偶数},\\ (3n+1)/2, & n\text{ 为奇数}. \end{cases}$$

每一次迭代记录一个奇偶位。

1.2 Syracuse 奇数映射

对奇数 $n$，令 $v(n)=\nu_2(3n+1)$，$S(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{v(n)}}$。则 $S(n)$ 仍为奇数，并且

$$S(n)=C^{v(n)}(n).$$

所以一个 Syracuse 区组不是固定长度的单步，它消耗 $v(n)$ 次 shortcut 迭代。记 $n_{i+1}=S(n_i)$，$v_i=v(n_i)$，$V_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i$，即前 $m$ 个区组中消去的因子 2 总数。

符号与技巧

符号$C(n)$
shortcut（加速）Collatz 映射：奇数时把"乘 3 加 1"和随后必然的一次除以 2 合成一步。每步恰好读一个奇偶位，这是 Terras 定理使用的时间单位。
符号$C^{v}$
$C$ 连续迭代 $v$ 次（函数的幂是复合，不是乘方）。$S(n)=C^{v(n)}(n)$：奇数步 1 次加偶数步 $v-1$ 次。
符号$V_m$
累计赋值，即前 $m$ 个区组一共除掉了多少个 2，也等于这些区组用掉的 shortcut 步数。
审读注记号冲突
本文用 $S(n)$ 表示映射，§3 又用 $S_i=\log_2\mu_i$ 表示对数增量。建议把后者改成 $X_i$ 或 $\ell_i$。§1.2 末尾"另记 $V_m$"也与前一句重复。

§2严格结果 I：赋值的自然密度分布

命题 2.1。对自然密度下均匀抽取的正奇数，$\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$，$\Pr(v\ge j)=2^{-(j-1)}$。

证明。$v\ge j$ 等价于 $3n+1\equiv0\pmod{2^j}$。3 模 $2^j$ 可逆，故有唯一剩余类解，且为奇数。奇数在模 $2^j$ 下有 $2^{j-1}$ 个剩余类，所以比例为 $2^{-(j-1)}$；相减得 $\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$。$\square$

推论 2.2。$\mathbb E[v]=\sum j/2^j=2$，$\mathbb E[v^2]=6$，$\operatorname{Var}(v)=2$。这些由剩余类计数精确得到，不是从模拟归纳的。

命题 2.3（有限字串的联合分布）。固定 $(j_0,\dots,j_{m-1})$，$j_i\ge1$，$J=\sum j_i$。前 $m$ 个赋值恰为该字串的奇数，在全体正奇数中的自然密度为
$$2^{-J}.$$
因此在奇数 2-adic Haar 概率空间中（忽略满足 $3n+1=0$ 的唯一零测点），$v_0,v_1,\dots$ 是 i.i.d. 几何变量。

证明思路。指定 $v_0=j_0$ 把起点限制在一个模 $2^{j_0+1}$ 的奇剩余类。之后下一状态是原起点的仿射函数，指定 $v_1=j_1$ 又固定 $j_1$ 个低位，依此类推。共固定 $J$ 个额外低位，密度为 $2^{-J}$。

关键限定。这是关于"初值总体"的密度陈述，不是说某个固定正整数的轨迹每一步都重新抽样。

符号与技巧

术语自然密度
满足条件的数在 $1..N$ 中所占比例当 $N\to\infty$ 的极限。"概率"指对起点均匀抽样。
符号2-adic Haar 测度
$\mathbb Z_2$ 上的"均匀分布"：二进制每一位都是独立的公平硬币。模 $2^k$ 的每个剩余类测度都是 $2^{-k}$。
术语柱集
只限定前有限个坐标的集合，例如"前 $m$ 个赋值等于给定字串"。测度论里先在柱集上定义概率，再扩展到整个空间。
技巧逐步固定低位
每指定一个赋值，就把起点限制到更细的剩余类。指定 $v=j$ 相当于固定 $j$ 个新的二进制位，所以概率逐项相乘，得到独立性。
术语仿射函数
形如 $an+b$ 的函数。固定前几步的赋值后，$n_k = (3^k n + c)/2^{V_k}$ 关于起点 $n$ 是仿射的。
审读注出处
命题 2.3 是 Terras (1976) 与 Everett (1977) 的经典结果在 Syracuse 坐标下的写法，Lagarias (1985) 的综述里也有，应注明。

§3严格结果 II：简化倍率的漂移与矩

暂时忽略 $+1$，定义简化倍率 $\mu_i=3\cdot2^{-v_i}$，$S_i=\log_2\mu_i=\log_2 3-v_i$。这是随机模型倍率，不是真实倍率的精确表达式。

3.1 算术平均与对数平均

$$\mathbb E[\mu]=3\sum_{j\ge1}4^{-j}=1,\qquad \mathbb E[S]=\log_2 3-2=-0.4150375\ldots,\qquad \exp(\mathbb E[\ln\mu])=\tfrac34 .$$

3.2 修正"矩方法失败"

对任意 $0<\theta<1$，

$$\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]=3^\theta\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}2^{-\theta j}=\frac{3^\theta}{2^{1+\theta}-1}=:q(\theta),\qquad q(0)=q(1)=1,\quad q(\theta)<1\ (0<\theta<1).$$

最后一个不等式由 $\log q$ 的严格凸性推出：它在 0 与 1 处为零，所以在两点之间严格小于 0。因此在独立模型里，一阶矩等于 1，分数阶矩却收缩。一阶矩本身不足以证明收缩；分数阶矩对随机模型有用，但不能直接升级为对每个确定性轨迹的结论。

3.3 Lundberg 系数与最大值界

$\mathbb E[2^{\theta S}]=q(\theta)$，正根为 $\theta=1$。在独立模型中 $M_m=2^{\sum_{i<m}S_i}$ 是非负鞅，于是

$$\Pr\Big(\sup_{m\ge0}M_m\ge y\Big)\le\frac1y .$$

这只对模型乘积成立。真实倍率是 $\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$，多出了依赖状态的正修正项 $1/n_i$。

符号与技巧

符号$q(\theta)$、分数阶矩
$\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]$，$\theta$ 可以不是整数。它和 $\mathbb E[2^{\theta S}]$ 是同一个量。
技巧对数凸性
矩母函数的对数 $\log\mathbb E[e^{\theta X}]$ 是 $\theta$ 的凸函数（由 Hölder 不等式）。凸函数在两个零点之间严格为负，所以只要知道 $q(0)=q(1)=1$ 且 $q$ 不是常数，就得到中间小于 1。
技巧分数阶矩法
若 $\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]=q<1$，则 $\mathbb E[M_m^\theta]=q^m\to0$，再用 Markov 不等式得到 $\Pr(M_m>\varepsilon)\le q^m/\varepsilon^\theta$，指数衰减。这就是一阶矩临界时仍能证明收缩的办法。
术语非负鞅
满足 $\mathbb E[M_{m+1}\mid\text{过去}]=M_m$ 的非负过程，即"公平赌局"下的资金。因为 $\mathbb E[\mu]=1$，乘积 $M_m$ 是鞅。
技巧Ville 极大不等式
非负鞅在整个时间轴上的上确界超过 $y$ 的概率不超过 $\mathbb E[M_0]/y$。这里 $M_0=1$，得到 $1/y$。
符号$\sup$
上确界，可以理解为整个序列能达到的最大值（可能取不到）。

§4严格结果 III：真实轨迹的精确对数恒等式

对真实轨迹，$\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3n_i+1}{2^{v_i}n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$。取对数并累加：

$$\ln\frac{n_m}{n_0}=m\ln3-(\ln2)V_m+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\ln\Big(1+\frac1{3n_i}\Big).$$

三项分别是：每个区组乘 3 的增长；全部二分带来的收缩；$+1$ 的精确修正。所以"平均 $v=2$"本身不是真实轨迹收缩的完整证明，还必须控制末项，并说明平均规律对哪一类轨迹成立。

符号与技巧

技巧望远镜乘积
$\frac{n_m}{n_0}=\prod_{i<m}\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}$，中间项全部约掉。取对数后成为可逐项控制的和。
技巧分离修正项
把 $3+1/n$ 写成 $3(1+\frac1{3n})$，主项与修正项分开。修正项为正且随 $n$ 增大而变小：$n\ge3$ 时每项不超过 $\ln(10/9)\approx0.105$。
符号$\ln$ 与 $\log_2$
本节用自然对数，其他节用以 2 为底。两者只差常数因子 $\ln2\approx0.693$。

§5核心引理：不下降轨迹必须缺少足够的二分

定理 5.1（下降证书与必要条件）。设 $n_0>1$ 为奇数，$n_{i+1}=S(n_i)$。

(A) 有限下降证书。若 $n_i\ge n_0$ 对所有 $0\le i<m$ 成立，且某个 $m\ge1$ 满足

$$V_m>m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big),$$

则 $n_m<n_0$。

(B) 不下降的必要条件。若对所有 $m$ 都有 $n_m\ge n_0$，则每个 $m\ge1$ 都满足

$$\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big).$$

特别地，$n_0\ge3$ 时右端 $\le\log_2(10/3)\approx1.7369656<2$；$n_0\to\infty$ 时趋向 $\log_2 3\approx1.5849625$。

证明。在 $n_i\ge n_0$ 的前缀里，$\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}\le\frac{3+1/n_0}{2^{v_i}}$。相乘得 $\frac{n_m}{n_0}\le\frac{(3+1/n_0)^m}{2^{V_m}}$。若 $V_m>m\log_2(3+1/n_0)$，右边小于 1，得 (A)。若轨迹始终不低于起点，左边至少为 1，反过来得到 (B)。$\square$

这条定理能做什么。它把"因子 2 累计得够不够多"变成可判定的有限前缀条件。若能证明每个奇数 $n_0>1$ 都会出现满足 (A) 的前缀，则每个起点都会降到自身以下，再由强归纳推出每个正整数到达 1。但目前还没有证明这样的前缀一定出现，所以这是严格的下降判据，不是完整证明。

实验台：看证书何时触发

纵轴是 $V_m-m\log_2 3$，即累计赋值比"刚好不增不减"多出多少。证书门槛是一条斜率极小的直线 $m\,(\log_2(3+1/n_0)-\log_2 3)$。黑线越过门槛（蓝线）时证书触发；竖线标出轨迹实际首次低于 $n_0$ 的区组。

V_m − m·log₂3证书门槛0（log₂3 线）实际首次下降

在 3 到 399,999 的全部 199,999 个奇数起点中，证书恰好在实际下降的那个区组触发的有 199,996 个。27、31、63 是例外：它们下降了，证书却没有触发，因为门槛用的是最坏情况 $3+1/n_0$，而真实修正项 $1/n_i$ 更小。

符号与技巧

技巧逐项放大取最坏情况
在"尚未下降"的前提下 $n_i\ge n_0$，于是 $1/n_i\le1/n_0$，每一项都能用同一个常数控制，乘起来得到干净的上界。代价是界变松，所以证书只是充分条件。
技巧逆否
(B) 是 (A) 的逆否命题：如果永远不下降，那么证书条件永远不成立。
技巧强归纳法
假设所有小于 $n_0$ 的数都到达 1，证明 $n_0$ 也到达 1。只要 $n_0$ 的轨迹进入某个更小的数，就沿用那个数的结论。所以"每个数都会降到自身以下"就足以推出整个猜想。
术语停止时间
轨迹首次低于起点的步数 $\sigma(n)$。Collatz 猜想等价于对所有 $n>1$，$\sigma(n)<\infty$。
审读注与经典结果的关系
Terras 定义了"系数停止时间" $\tau(n)$：shortcut 轨迹中 $3^{(\text{奇数步数})}/2^{(\text{总步数})}$ 首次小于 1 的时刻，并证明 $\tau(n)\le\sigma(n)$。定理 5.1 是同一思路加上 $+1$ 的显式修正，应在文中说明这一关系。

§6推论：最小反例与循环都是异常的低赋值轨迹

假设猜想为假，令 $n_0$ 为最小的不能到达 1 的奇数起点。若轨迹中有任何奇数状态低于 $n_0$，按最小性它会到达 1，矛盾。所以 $n_i\ge n_0$ 对所有 $i$ 成立，由定理 5.1，

$$\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big)<2\qquad\text{对所有 }m.$$

最小反例不能有随机模型的典型平均赋值 2，它必须属于持续偏低的特殊集合。

6.1 若轨迹始终 ≥ 3 且平均赋值趋近 2

此时 $\ln(1+\frac1{3n_i})\le\ln(10/9)$，于是 $\ln\frac{n_m}{n_0}\le m\ln3-V_m\ln2+m\ln(10/9)$。若 $V_m/m\to2$，除以 $m$ 的上极限不超过 $\ln3-2\ln2+\ln(10/9)=\ln(5/6)<0$，$n_m$ 指数下降，矛盾。

6.2 非平凡循环

若 $n_m=n_0$，则 $1=\prod_{i<m}\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$，故

$$\frac{V_m}{m}=\frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_i}\Big),\qquad \log_2 3<\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_{\min}}\Big).$$

非平凡正奇数循环有 $n_{\min}\ge3$，所以平均赋值必须落在 $1.5849625\ldots<V_m/m\le1.7369656\ldots$，而不是 2。负漂移的随机直觉不能单独排除循环。

符号与技巧

技巧最小反例法
假设有反例，取其中最小的一个，利用"最小"推出额外性质（这里是轨迹永不低于起点），再找矛盾或刻画它。
符号$\limsup$（上极限）
序列尾部能反复接近的最大值。上极限为负表示最终一直为负，于是 $\ln(n_m/n_0)\to-\infty$。
技巧循环的乘积恒等式
绕一圈回到原点，所有倍率之积等于 1。这把"存在循环"转成 $2^{V_m}$ 与 $\prod(3+1/n_i)$ 的精确相等，是排除循环的标准起点。
审读注可以更强
已验证所有 $n<2^{71}$ 都到达 1，所以非平凡循环有 $n_{\min}>2^{71}$，上界变为 $\log_2 3+O(2^{-71})$。于是 $2^{V_m}$ 必须极其接近 $3^m$，再结合 $\log_23$ 的连分数可推出循环长度的巨大下界（Eliahou 1993，Simons–de Weger 2005）。另外 §6.1 已被 (B) 包含，可以删去。

§7为什么概率论还不能给出"100% 不发散"

7.1 概率模型能严格证明的

在奇数 2-adic Haar 空间中 $v_i$ i.i.d.，$\mathbb E[v_i]=2$。由强大数定律，几乎处处 $\frac1m\sum_{i<m}v_i\to2$。所以对任何固定 $c<2$，几乎每个赋值字串都会在某个有限前缀满足 $V_m/m>c$。但 2-adic 点没有与正整数相同的阿基米德大小关系，这不能直接说明某个给定正整数一定下降。

7.2 为什么这不排除每一个正整数

正整数在 $\mathbb Z_2$ 中是可数集，每个单点测度为零，所以全体正整数的 Haar 测度为零。"Haar 几乎每个初值满足某规律"推不出"每个正整数满足该规律"。下面两件事也不能混为一谈：

  • 对每个固定前缀，随初值范围扩大，赋值字串趋于独立分布；
  • 对每个固定初值，沿无限长轨迹的平均赋值趋于 2。

它们的极限次序不同。Terras–Everett 证明几乎所有正整数在有限时间内降到起点以下；Korec 证明对任意 $c>\log_4 3$，几乎所有起点会低于 $n^c$；Tao 在对数密度下证明几乎所有轨迹会低于任意趋于无穷的函数。它们都很强，但都不是"每个正整数"。

7.3 原假设 7.2 需要重写

原"比特强制耦合引理"没有定义差异、样本空间、均匀性的对象，也没说常数是否对所有 $n,k$ 一致。若理解为"每条轨迹的后续赋值都保持几何分布"，则显然不成立：$S(1)=1$，$v(1)=2$，起点 1 的赋值序列是 $2,2,2,\dots$。建议改写为可验证的桥接命题：

异常轨迹排除问题。不存在正奇数 $n_0>1$，使得其 Syracuse 赋值字串对所有 $m\ge1$ 都满足
$$\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i\le m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big).$$

若能证明它，定理 5.1 立即给出每个起点的有限下降，再由强归纳推出猜想。但这个命题实质上已经非常接近经典停止时间问题本身。

符号与技巧

术语强大数定律
i.i.d. 变量的样本均值以概率 1 收敛到期望。"以概率 1"允许一个测度为零的例外集合。
术语几乎处处
除去一个测度为零的集合外都成立。正整数本身就在这样的集合里，所以"几乎处处"对正整数什么也没保证。
技巧可数集测度为零
每个单点的 Haar 测度为 0（它落在每个模 $2^k$ 的剩余类里，测度 $\le2^{-k}$）。可数个零相加仍为零。
术语阿基米德大小
通常意义下数的大小 $|x|_\infty$。2-adic 世界只有 $|x|_2$，"下降到起点以下"在那里没有意义，这是概率结论无法直接搬回整数的原因之一。
技巧极限次序
"先固定 $m$ 再让 $N\to\infty$"和"先固定 $n$ 再让 $m\to\infty$"是两个不同的命题。交换极限需要一致性估计，而这正是缺的部分。
审读注桥接命题的强度
该命题蕴含猜想；反过来，若某个 $n_0$ 永不下降，它就满足条件 (B)。所以它与"每个 $n>1$ 的停止时间有限"几乎等价，是对障碍的精确重述，难度没有降低。

§8v0.1 比特预算的修正

v0.1 用 $\frac{\log_2 n}{2-\log_2 3}\approx2.409\log_2 n$ 估计区组数。这是让模型对数尺度减少约 $\log_2 n$ 所需的区组数，不是"到达 1 的精确步数"：进入小数区间后 $+1$ 不可忽略，$S(1)=1$ 的不动点也要单独处理。

更关键的是，一个区组长 $v_i$ 个 shortcut 步，$\mathbb E[v_i]=2$，所以总 shortcut 步数约为

$$2\cdot2.409\log_2 n\approx4.819\log_2 n .$$

Terras 定理控制的是 shortcut 步的奇偶位前缀。只用约 $\log_2 n$ 个初始奇偶位，对应约 $\tfrac12\log_2 n$ 个区组，剩余规模为

$$\log_2 n_{\mathrm{res}}\approx\log_2 n-\tfrac12(2-\log_2 3)\log_2 n=\tfrac12\log_2 3\,\log_2 n,\qquad n_{\mathrm{res}}\approx n^{\log_4 3}\approx n^{0.79248}.$$

这个指数与 Korec 的结果一致：对每个 $c>\log_4 3$，几乎所有起点会降到 $n^c$ 以下。这只是解释同一指数为何自然出现，不是 Korec 定理的证明。还要把这一点表述为"统计模型可控制的奇偶前缀长度"，而不是"整数只携带这些位、之后就不能有规律"。

符号与技巧

符号$\log_4 3$
$=\frac{\log_2 3}{2}\approx0.7925$，即 $4^{0.7925}=3$。它是"每两步乘 3、除 4"的平衡指数。
技巧量纲检查
比较两个量之前先确认单位一致。v0.1 的错误是拿"区组"和"比特"直接比较，而一个区组平均消耗 2 比特。
术语Korec 定理
Korec (1994)：对任意 $c>\log_4 3$，几乎所有 $n$ 的轨迹会到达小于 $n^c$ 的值。这是 Terras 型论证在 $\log_2 n$ 步内能达到的最佳指数。
符号$n_{\mathrm{res}}$
"剩余规模"：用完初始比特的保证之后，轨迹典型地停在的大小。

§10继续研究的路线

路线 A：把下降证书形式化。定理 5.1 与强归纳推论是初等的，可以完全放进 Lean 或 Isabelle，但它不会自动证明证书总会出现。

路线 B：精确定义异常赋值字串。对初值 $n_0$ 定义

$$\mathcal E(n_0)=\Big\{(v_i)_{i\ge0}:\ \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i\le m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big)\ \text{对所有 }m\ge1\Big\}.$$

每条不低于起点的轨迹都编码为 $\mathcal E(n_0)$ 中的字串。i.i.d. 模型说明这类字串是零测集；真正要证明的是没有正整数的轨迹落入其中。不要把"测度为零"当成"空集"。

路线 C：用固定前缀检验概率结构。对每个固定区组索引 $j$，从同一批奇数起点计算第 $j$ 个赋值的分布及联合字串频率，避免首次下降截断带来的选择效应；不要让 $m$ 以未经证明的方式随 $\log n$ 增长。

路线 D：把 H1 与 H2 分开。H1 要排除不下降且无界的轨迹；H2 要排除所有不经过 1 的循环。周期问题还涉及 $2^V$ 对 $3^m$ 的逼近，需要额外的数论控制。

符号与技巧

符号$\mathcal E(n_0)$
异常集合：所有前缀平均赋值都不超过门槛的无限字串。它依赖 $n_0$，因为门槛 $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$ 依赖 $n_0$。
技巧编码
把整数轨迹映成一个赋值字串，再问"哪些字串能由正整数实现"。难点在于整数的字串由确定的算术决定，不由抽样决定。
术语Lean / Isabelle
交互式定理证明器，每一步推理都由计算机检查。

§12结论

本文给出的严格进展不是完整证明，而是以下组合：

  1. 在自然密度/2-adic 模型中，有限前缀的赋值字串确实精确服从独立几何分布；简化倍率模型的对数漂移为负。
  2. 真实整数轨迹有精确的下降证书：在尚未低于起点的前提下，若累计平均赋值越过 $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$，当前末端必低于起点。
  3. 所有不下降的最小反例都必须在每个前缀维持低于约 1.585–1.737 的平均赋值，而典型值是 2。
  4. 难点：这类异常字串在 Haar 意义下是零测的，但还不能证明它们不含任何正整数轨迹。从"零测异常集合"到"没有正整数落入其中"就是缺少的确定性桥梁。

本文没有证明"100% 不发散"，但把目标缩减为一个精确的全称命题，并修正了 v0.1 比特预算的单位。下一步应当研究异常赋值字串与正整数编码之间的约束，而不是继续从几何分布的均值推导全称收敛。

Abstract

We study the Syracuse odd-compression map $S(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{v(n)}}$, $v(n)=\nu_2(3n+1)\ge1$, for positive odd $n$. The intuition under test: although the first halving in each block is forced and the multiplier may still be about $3/2$, the accumulated halvings might force the system downwards on a longer time scale.

The note obtains five rigorous conclusions:

  1. Over odd integers in natural density, $\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$; more strongly, any fixed finite valuation string has density $2^{-\sum v_i}$, corresponding to i.i.d. geometric variables in the odd 2-adic Haar probability space.
  2. In the simplified multiplier model $\mu=3\cdot2^{-v}$, the arithmetic mean multiplier is 1, the log drift is $\log_2 3-2<0$, and every fractional moment with $0<\theta<1$ satisfies $\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]<1$. Being critical in the first moment does not mean every moment method fails.
  3. Real orbits have an exact descent criterion: if the orbit has not yet gone below $n_0$ and the current prefix’s mean valuation exceeds $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$, the current endpoint is below $n_0$.
  4. Any orbit that never goes below its start $n_0>1$ satisfies $\frac1m\sum_{i<m}v_i\le\log_2(3+\frac1{n_0})<2$ on every prefix. This is the “low accumulated halving” condition any minimal counterexample or nontrivial cycle must meet.
  5. Statistical independence and the strong law of large numbers show only that typical 2-adic starting points cannot satisfy this low-average condition forever; positive integers form a null set under 2-adic Haar measure, so a special positive integer is not excluded. What is missing is a way to lift statistical regularity to deterministic control of every positive integer’s orbit.

In addition, the bit-budget calculation in §7 of v0.1 needs correcting: a Syracuse block corresponds to $v_i$ iterations of the shortcut map, not exactly one. $2.409\log_2 n$ blocks correspond to about $4.819\log_2 n$ shortcut iterations; using only about $\log_2 n$ initial parity bits, the model’s residual exponent is $\log_4 3\approx0.79248$, matching Korec’s classical result. This is heuristic agreement, not a new proof.

§1Definitions and notation

To avoid confusing “ordinary steps” with “odd blocks”, two maps are used.

1.1 The shortcut map

$$C(n)=\begin{cases} n/2, & n\text{ even},\\ (3n+1)/2, & n\text{ odd}. \end{cases}$$

Each iteration records one parity bit.

1.2 The Syracuse odd map

For odd $n$, let $v(n)=\nu_2(3n+1)$ and $S(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{v(n)}}$. Then $S(n)$ is odd and

$$S(n)=C^{v(n)}(n).$$

So a Syracuse block is not a fixed-length step; it uses $v(n)$ shortcut iterations. Write $n_{i+1}=S(n_i)$, $v_i=v(n_i)$, $V_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i$, the total number of factors of 2 removed in the first $m$ blocks.

Notation and techniques

notation$C(n)$
The shortcut (accelerated) Collatz map: for odd numbers it merges “times 3 plus 1” with the halving that must follow. Each step reads exactly one parity bit; this is the time unit Terras’ theorem uses.
notation$C^{v}$
$C$ iterated $v$ times (a power of a function means composition, not exponentiation). $S(n)=C^{v(n)}(n)$: one odd step plus $v-1$ even steps.
notation$V_m$
Cumulative valuation: how many factors of 2 the first $m$ blocks removed in total, which is also the number of shortcut steps they used.
review noteNotation clash
The note uses $S(n)$ for the map, and §3 also uses $S_i=\log_2\mu_i$ for the log increment. Suggest renaming the latter $X_i$ or $\ell_i$. $V_m$ is also defined twice at the end of §1.2.

§2Rigorous result I: natural density of valuations

Proposition 2.1. For positive odd integers drawn uniformly in natural density, $\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$ and $\Pr(v\ge j)=2^{-(j-1)}$.

Proof. $v\ge j$ is equivalent to $3n+1\equiv0\pmod{2^j}$. Since 3 is invertible mod $2^j$, there is a unique residue class of solutions, and it is odd. Odd numbers occupy $2^{j-1}$ residue classes mod $2^j$, so the proportion is $2^{-(j-1)}$; differencing gives $\Pr(v=j)=2^{-j}$. $\square$

Corollary 2.2. $\mathbb E[v]=\sum j/2^j=2$, $\mathbb E[v^2]=6$, $\operatorname{Var}(v)=2$. These follow exactly from counting residue classes, not from extrapolating simulations.

Proposition 2.3 (joint law of finite strings). Fix $(j_0,\dots,j_{m-1})$ with $j_i\ge1$ and $J=\sum j_i$. The odd integers whose first $m$ valuations equal this string have natural density
$$2^{-J}$$
among all positive odd integers. Hence in the odd 2-adic Haar probability space (ignoring the single null point with $3n+1=0$), $v_0,v_1,\dots$ are i.i.d. geometric variables.

Proof sketch. Specifying $v_0=j_0$ restricts the start to one odd residue class mod $2^{j_0+1}$. After that, the next state is an affine function of the original start, and specifying $v_1=j_1$ fixes $j_1$ more low bits, and so on. In total $J$ extra low bits are fixed, giving density $2^{-J}$.

Key caveat. This is a statement about the population of starting values, not a claim that a fixed positive integer’s orbit is resampled at each step.

Notation and techniques

termNatural density
The limit as $N\to\infty$ of the proportion of numbers in $1..N$ satisfying a condition. “Probability” means uniformly sampling the start.
notation2-adic Haar measure
The “uniform distribution” on $\mathbb Z_2$: every binary digit is an independent fair coin. Each residue class mod $2^k$ has measure $2^{-k}$.
termCylinder set
A set constrained only on finitely many coordinates, e.g. “the first $m$ valuations equal a given string”. Measure theory defines probabilities on cylinders first, then extends them to the whole space.
techniqueFixing low bits step by step
Each specified valuation restricts the start to a finer residue class. Specifying $v=j$ fixes $j$ new binary digits, so probabilities multiply, giving independence.
termAffine function
A function of the form $an+b$. Once the first few valuations are fixed, $n_k = (3^k n + c)/2^{V_k}$ is affine in the start $n$.
review noteAttribution
Proposition 2.3 is the classical result of Terras (1976) and Everett (1977) written in Syracuse coordinates; it also appears in Lagarias’ 1985 survey and should be cited.

§3Rigorous result II: drift and moments of the simplified multiplier

Ignoring the $+1$ for now, define the simplified multiplier $\mu_i=3\cdot2^{-v_i}$ and $S_i=\log_2\mu_i=\log_2 3-v_i$. This is the random-model multiplier, not an exact expression for the real one.

3.1 Arithmetic and log averages

$$\mathbb E[\mu]=3\sum_{j\ge1}4^{-j}=1,\qquad \mathbb E[S]=\log_2 3-2=-0.4150375\ldots,\qquad \exp(\mathbb E[\ln\mu])=\tfrac34 .$$

3.2 Correcting “moment methods fail”

For any $0<\theta<1$,

$$\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]=3^\theta\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}2^{-\theta j}=\frac{3^\theta}{2^{1+\theta}-1}=:q(\theta),\qquad q(0)=q(1)=1,\quad q(\theta)<1\ (0<\theta<1).$$

The last inequality follows from the strict convexity of $\log q$: it vanishes at 0 and 1, so it is strictly negative between them. In the independent model the first moment equals 1 while fractional moments contract. The first moment alone cannot prove contraction; fractional moments help in the random model but cannot be lifted directly to every deterministic orbit.

3.3 Lundberg coefficient and maximal bound

$\mathbb E[2^{\theta S}]=q(\theta)$, with positive root $\theta=1$. In the independent model $M_m=2^{\sum_{i<m}S_i}$ is a nonnegative martingale, so

$$\Pr\Big(\sup_{m\ge0}M_m\ge y\Big)\le\frac1y .$$

This holds only for the model product. The real multiplier is $\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$, with an extra state-dependent positive term $1/n_i$.

Notation and techniques

notation$q(\theta)$, fractional moments
$\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]$ where $\theta$ need not be an integer. It is the same quantity as $\mathbb E[2^{\theta S}]$.
techniqueLog-convexity
The log of a moment-generating function, $\log\mathbb E[e^{\theta X}]$, is convex in $\theta$ (by Hölder’s inequality). A convex function is strictly negative between two zeros, so $q(0)=q(1)=1$ with $q$ non-constant gives $q<1$ in between.
techniqueFractional moment method
If $\mathbb E[\mu^\theta]=q<1$, then $\mathbb E[M_m^\theta]=q^m\to0$, and Markov’s inequality gives $\Pr(M_m>\varepsilon)\le q^m/\varepsilon^\theta$, exponential decay. This is how contraction can be proved even when the first moment is critical.
termNonnegative martingale
A nonnegative process with $\mathbb E[M_{m+1}\mid\text{past}]=M_m$, like a gambler’s fortune in a fair game. Because $\mathbb E[\mu]=1$, the product $M_m$ is a martingale.
techniqueVille’s maximal inequality
The probability that a nonnegative martingale ever exceeds $y$ is at most $\mathbb E[M_0]/y$. Here $M_0=1$, giving $1/y$.
notation$\sup$
Supremum, roughly the largest value the sequence reaches (it may not be attained).

§4Rigorous result III: an exact log identity for real orbits

For a real orbit, $\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3n_i+1}{2^{v_i}n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$. Taking logs and summing:

$$\ln\frac{n_m}{n_0}=m\ln3-(\ln2)V_m+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\ln\Big(1+\frac1{3n_i}\Big).$$

The three terms are: growth from multiplying by 3 in each block; contraction from all the halvings; and the exact correction from the $+1$. So “average $v=2$” is not by itself a complete proof that real orbits contract; one must also control the last term and say which orbits the average law applies to.

Notation and techniques

techniqueTelescoping product
$\frac{n_m}{n_0}=\prod_{i<m}\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}$; all intermediate terms cancel. Taking logs gives a sum that can be controlled term by term.
techniqueSeparating the correction
Write $3+1/n$ as $3(1+\frac1{3n})$ to split the main term from the correction. The correction is positive and shrinks as $n$ grows: for $n\ge3$ each term is at most $\ln(10/9)\approx0.105$.
notation$\ln$ and $\log_2$
This section uses natural logs, the others base 2. They differ only by the constant factor $\ln2\approx0.693$.

§5Core lemma: non-descending orbits must lack enough halvings

Theorem 5.1 (descent certificate and necessary condition). Let $n_0>1$ be odd and $n_{i+1}=S(n_i)$.

(A) Finite descent certificate. If $n_i\ge n_0$ for all $0\le i<m$, and some $m\ge1$ satisfies

$$V_m>m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big),$$

then $n_m<n_0$.

(B) Necessary condition for never descending. If $n_m\ge n_0$ for all $m$, then every $m\ge1$ satisfies

$$\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big).$$

In particular, for $n_0\ge3$ the right side is $\le\log_2(10/3)\approx1.7369656<2$; as $n_0\to\infty$ it tends to $\log_2 3\approx1.5849625$.

Proof. On a prefix with $n_i\ge n_0$, $\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i}=\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}\le\frac{3+1/n_0}{2^{v_i}}$. Multiplying gives $\frac{n_m}{n_0}\le\frac{(3+1/n_0)^m}{2^{V_m}}$. If $V_m>m\log_2(3+1/n_0)$ the right side is below 1, giving (A). If the orbit never goes below its start, the left side is at least 1, giving (B). $\square$

What the theorem does. It turns “are there enough accumulated factors of 2” into a decidable condition on finite prefixes. If one could prove that every odd $n_0>1$ has a prefix satisfying (A), every start would fall below itself, and strong induction would give that every positive integer reaches 1. But no one has shown such a prefix must appear, so this is a rigorous descent criterion, not a complete proof.

Notation and techniques

techniqueBounding each term by the worst case
While the orbit has “not yet descended”, $n_i\ge n_0$, so $1/n_i\le1/n_0$ and every term is controlled by the same constant, giving a clean product bound. The cost is a looser bound, so the certificate is only sufficient.
techniqueContrapositive
(B) is the contrapositive of (A): if the orbit never descends, the certificate condition never holds.
techniqueStrong induction
Assume every number below $n_0$ reaches 1 and prove $n_0$ does too. As soon as $n_0$’s orbit enters a smaller number, that number’s result carries over. So “every number falls below itself” is enough to give the whole conjecture.
termStopping time
The number of steps until the orbit first goes below its start, $\sigma(n)$. The Collatz conjecture is equivalent to $\sigma(n)<\infty$ for all $n>1$.
review noteRelation to classical results
Terras defined the “coefficient stopping time” $\tau(n)$: the first time in the shortcut orbit when $3^{(\text{odd steps})}/2^{(\text{total steps})}$ drops below 1, and proved $\tau(n)\le\sigma(n)$. Theorem 5.1 is the same idea with an explicit correction for the $+1$; the note should say so.

§6Corollary: minimal counterexamples and cycles are anomalous low-valuation orbits

Suppose the conjecture is false and let $n_0$ be the least odd start that never reaches 1. If its orbit contains any odd state below $n_0$, minimality says that state reaches 1, and so does $n_0$, a contradiction. So $n_i\ge n_0$ for all $i$, and by Theorem 5.1,

$$\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big)<2\qquad\text{for all }m.$$

A minimal counterexample cannot have the random model’s typical mean valuation 2; it must belong to a special set with persistently low values.

6.1 An orbit that stays ≥ 3 with mean valuation tending to 2

Then $\ln(1+\frac1{3n_i})\le\ln(10/9)$, so $\ln\frac{n_m}{n_0}\le m\ln3-V_m\ln2+m\ln(10/9)$. If $V_m/m\to2$, the limsup of the right side divided by $m$ is at most $\ln3-2\ln2+\ln(10/9)=\ln(5/6)<0$, so $n_m$ decreases exponentially, a contradiction.

6.2 Nontrivial cycles

If $n_m=n_0$, then $1=\prod_{i<m}\frac{3+1/n_i}{2^{v_i}}$, so

$$\frac{V_m}{m}=\frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_i}\Big),\qquad \log_2 3<\frac{V_m}{m}\le\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_{\min}}\Big).$$

A nontrivial positive odd cycle has $n_{\min}\ge3$, so its mean valuation must lie in $1.5849625\ldots<V_m/m\le1.7369656\ldots$, not at 2. Negative-drift intuition alone cannot exclude cycles.

Notation and techniques

techniqueMinimal counterexample
Assume a counterexample exists, take the smallest one, and use minimality to derive extra properties (here, the orbit never goes below its start), then find a contradiction or characterise it.
notation$\limsup$
The largest value the tail of a sequence keeps returning to. A negative limsup means the sequence is eventually always negative, so $\ln(n_m/n_0)\to-\infty$.
techniqueThe product identity of a cycle
Going once round a cycle, the product of all multipliers is 1. This turns “a cycle exists” into an exact equality between $2^{V_m}$ and $\prod(3+1/n_i)$, the standard starting point for excluding cycles.

Then $2^{V_m}$ must be extraordinarily close to $3^m$, and with the continued fraction of $\log_23$ this gives huge lower bounds on cycle length (Eliahou 1993, Simons–de Weger 2005). Also, §6.1 is already covered by (B) and can be removed.

§7Why probability cannot yet give “100% no divergence”

7.1 What the probabilistic model proves rigorously

In the odd 2-adic Haar space the $v_i$ are i.i.d. with $\mathbb E[v_i]=2$. By the strong law of large numbers, almost surely $\frac1m\sum_{i<m}v_i\to2$. So for any fixed $c<2$, almost every valuation string has some finite prefix with $V_m/m>c$. But 2-adic points do not carry the archimedean ordering of positive integers, so this cannot directly show that a given positive integer’s orbit descends.

7.2 Why this does not cover every positive integer

The positive integers form a countable subset of $\mathbb Z_2$; each point has Haar measure zero, so all positive integers together have measure zero. “Haar-almost every start obeys a law” does not imply “every positive integer obeys it”. Nor should these two statements be confused:

  • for each fixed prefix, as the range of starts grows, the valuation strings tend to independence;
  • for each fixed start, the mean valuation along the infinite orbit tends to 2.

They take limits in different orders. Terras–Everett proved that almost all positive integers fall below their start in finite time; Korec proved that for any $c>\log_4 3$ almost all starts go below $n^c$; Tao proved in logarithmic density that almost all orbits go below any function tending to infinity. All are strong, and none covers every positive integer.

7.3 The original Hypothesis 7.2 needs rewriting

The original “bit-forcing coupling lemma” did not define discrepancy, the sample space, or what is uniform, nor whether the constant is uniform in $n,k$. Read as “every orbit’s later valuations stay geometrically distributed”, it is plainly false: $S(1)=1$ and $v(1)=2$, so the orbit of 1 has valuations $2,2,2,\dots$. The note proposes a checkable bridge statement instead:

Anomalous orbit exclusion problem. There is no odd positive integer $n_0>1$ whose Syracuse valuation string satisfies, for all $m\ge1$,
$$\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i\le m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big).$$

If this were proved, Theorem 5.1 would immediately give finite descent from every start, and strong induction would give the conjecture. But this statement is already very close to the classical stopping-time problem itself.

Notation and techniques

termStrong law of large numbers
The sample mean of i.i.d. variables converges to the expectation with probability 1. “With probability 1” allows an exceptional set of measure zero.
termAlmost everywhere
True except on a set of measure zero. The positive integers themselves lie in such a set, so “almost everywhere” guarantees nothing about them.
techniqueCountable sets have measure zero
Each single point has Haar measure 0 (it lies in a residue class mod $2^k$ of measure $\le2^{-k}$ for every $k$). Countably many zeros still sum to zero.
termArchimedean size
The usual size of a number, $|x|_\infty$. The 2-adic world has only $|x|_2$, where “falling below the start” has no meaning; this is one reason probabilistic results cannot be carried straight back to the integers.
techniqueOrder of limits
“Fix $m$, then let $N\to\infty$” and “fix $n$, then let $m\to\infty$” are different statements. Swapping limits needs a uniform estimate, which is exactly what is missing.
review noteStrength of the bridge statement
It implies the conjecture; conversely, any $n_0$ that never descends satisfies condition (B). So it is nearly equivalent to “every $n>1$ has finite stopping time”: an exact restatement of the obstruction, not a reduction in difficulty.

§8Correcting the v0.1 bit budget

v0.1 estimated the number of blocks as $\frac{\log_2 n}{2-\log_2 3}\approx2.409\log_2 n$. This is the number of blocks for the model’s log scale to drop by about $\log_2 n$, not “the exact number of steps to reach 1”: once the orbit reaches small values the $+1$ is not negligible, and the fixed point $S(1)=1$ needs separate treatment.

More importantly, a block is $v_i$ shortcut steps long and $\mathbb E[v_i]=2$, so the total number of shortcut steps is about

$$2\cdot2.409\log_2 n\approx4.819\log_2 n .$$

Terras’ theorem controls the parity prefix of shortcut steps. Using only about $\log_2 n$ initial parity bits covers about $\tfrac12\log_2 n$ blocks, leaving a residual size of

$$\log_2 n_{\mathrm{res}}\approx\log_2 n-\tfrac12(2-\log_2 3)\log_2 n=\tfrac12\log_2 3\,\log_2 n,\qquad n_{\mathrm{res}}\approx n^{\log_4 3}\approx n^{0.79248}.$$

This exponent matches Korec: for every $c>\log_4 3$, almost all starts fall below $n^c$. This only explains why the same exponent appears naturally; it is not a proof of Korec’s theorem. The point should also be stated as “the parity-prefix length the statistical model can control”, not “an integer carries only these bits and can have no regularity afterwards”.

  • $\mathbb E[v\mid v\ge2]=3$: exact from the geometric distribution.

9.2 Should be downgraded or rewritten

  1. “Moment methods fail”: change to “contraction cannot be derived from the first moment alone”.
  2. “No negative feedback obstructs every locally monotone Lyapunov function”: at most shows that the i.i.d. conditional-mean model has no compensation mechanism.
  3. “A terminating valuation of exactly 3 is a new feedback measurement”: in the i.i.d. geometric model it is 3 automatically, and simulation is just an implementation check.
  4. “A bit-budget gap of 2.41”: units must be multiplied by the mean block length 2; the exponent should be $\log_4 3$, not 0.585.
  5. “All positive-integer orbits follow this statistical law”: the probability space and order of limits must be made explicit.
  6. “Non-commutativity and the arrow of time come only from the $+1$”: acceptable as a heuristic, but the algebraic object must be defined first.

9.3 Recomputation

$\texttt{range(3, 400001, 2)}$ actually gives 199,999 starts and 696,730 blocks; $\mathbb E[v]\approx2.00123003$, simplified multiplier mean $\approx0.99972836$, drift $\approx-0.41626753$. Because orbits are cut at first descent, these blocks are not independent fixed-depth samples and only describe empirical statistics under that stopping rule. $v=20$ occurs at $349{,}525$, so $N=400{,}000$ should be used: $\log_2(3N)\approx20.19$, not the 19.78 from $N=300{,}000$.

§10Routes for further research

Route A: formalise the descent certificate. Theorem 5.1 and its strong-induction corollary are elementary and can be fully formalised in Lean or Isabelle, but that will not prove the certificate always appears.

Route B: define anomalous valuation strings precisely. For a start $n_0$ define

$$\mathcal E(n_0)=\Big\{(v_i)_{i\ge0}:\ \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}v_i\le m\log_2\Big(3+\frac1{n_0}\Big)\ \text{for all }m\ge1\Big\}.$$

Every orbit that never goes below its start encodes a string in $\mathcal E(n_0)$. The i.i.d. model says such strings form a null set; what must be proved is that no positive integer’s orbit lands in it. Do not treat “measure zero” as “empty”.

Route C: test the probabilistic structure with fixed prefixes. For each fixed block index $j$, compute the distribution of the $j$-th valuation and the joint string frequencies over the same batch of odd starts, avoiding the selection effect of truncating at first descent; do not let $m$ grow with $\log n$ in an unproven way.

Route D: separate H1 and H2. H1 must exclude orbits that never descend and are unbounded; H2 must exclude every cycle not passing through 1. Cycles also involve how well $2^V$ approximates $3^m$, which needs extra number-theoretic control.

notation$\mathcal E(n_0)$
The anomalous set: all infinite strings whose prefix means never exceed the threshold. It depends on $n_0$ because the threshold $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$ does.
techniqueEncoding
Map an integer orbit to a valuation string, then ask which strings can be realised by positive integers. The difficulty is that an integer’s string is fixed by exact arithmetic, not by sampling.

Interactive theorem provers; every inference is checked by computer.

§12Conclusion

The rigorous progress offered here is not a complete proof but the following combination:

  1. In the natural-density / 2-adic model, finite valuation prefixes really are exactly independent and geometric; the simplified multiplier model has negative log drift.
  2. Real integer orbits have an exact descent certificate: provided the orbit has not yet gone below its start, if the accumulated mean valuation exceeds $\log_2(3+1/n_0)$, the current endpoint is below the start.
  3. Every non-descending minimal counterexample must keep its mean valuation below roughly 1.585–1.737 on every prefix, while the typical value is 2.
  4. The difficulty: probability says such anomalous strings have Haar measure zero, but cannot prove that they contain no positive-integer orbit. Getting from “a null anomalous set” to “no positive integer lands in it” is the missing deterministic bridge.

This note does not prove “100% no divergence”, but it reduces the goal to one precise universal statement and corrects the units of v0.1’s bit budget. Next steps should study the constraints linking anomalous valuation strings to the encoding of positive integers, rather than continuing to derive universal convergence from the mean of the geometric distribution.
